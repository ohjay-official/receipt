## What prior official write-ups say the judges rewarded

These quotes are from TestMu’s own recap of the **August 2026 online** edition, which used the **same four dimensions and three bars**. They are the best available window onto what a TestMu engineer panel actually scored up, not a promise that the same people will sit this time.

From [The Kane CLI Hackathon Winners](https://www.testmuai.com/blog/kane-cli-hackathon-winners/) (published 19 Sep 2026):

- On demo.studio (1st): “Sam did not use Kane CLI once. He used it three ways… as a build partner… as the performer… as the tester… That layered use is what the judges rewarded. Kane CLI was not bolted on. It was the build loop, the runtime, and the safety net at once.”
- On GuardianKane (2nd): “This is the pattern we built Kane CLI for. The agent builds it. Kane CLI proves it works.” The write-up highlights a Stop-hook that will not let Claude mark a task done until Kane has replayed a test and done a defect sweep, plus a companion demo that “catches a real checkout bug, the agent fixes it, Kane re-verifies.”
- On KANE vs. ABLE (3rd): “The most creative entry.” “This is a closed verification loop in its purest form. Builder claims. Verifier tests. Failure produces evidence. Evidence drives the fix. Verifier confirms the fix.” “Kane was not scripted through the dungeon. It solved it.”
- Shared pattern: “None of them treated Kane CLI as a test runner sitting at the end of a pipeline.” “In every case, the output is the same thing: a pass or fail backed by evidence from a real browser. Not a claim. Proof.”

The official prompt on this edition’s Luma page is almost identical in spirit:

> “A polished todo app with one Kane flow tacked on at the end will lose to a weirder, scrappier thing where a hook fires Kane and the agent re-prompts itself based on what Kane finds. Both are valid submissions. The second is the one we're really hoping to see.”  
> Source: [Luma](https://luma.com/kanecli-online-hackathon)
